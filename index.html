Why SMITHY?

The name

A smithy is the smith's workshop, where metal is shaped through skill, heat and force. We shape materials with jets, spindles, sensors and physics-informed models. SMITHY stands for Smart Manufacturing through Intelligent Twins and HYbrid Machining.

The wordmark

The I is an abrasive waterjet passing through a gear inside a ring of sensor nodes and striking an anvil, with the anvil's digital twin drawn below. The Y joins an end mill and a waterjet into one tool: hybrid machining.

The monogram

Seen whole, it is a gantry machine tool: columns, beam, spindle, jet, bed and toolpath. Look closer and its strokes spell all six letters, S, M, I, T, H and Y.

Research

From process physics to self-correcting machines

We build hybrid computational frameworks that combine first-principles engineering, analytical modelling, computational physics and AI/ML. The goal is reliable digital twins of manufacturing processes and machines, and through them, predictive and autonomous manufacturing for aerospace, defence, automotive and biomedical industries.

Thread 1

Abrasive waterjet milling

Core strength

Controlled-depth milling of Ti-6Al-4V with abrasive waterjets. We couple CFD-simulated jet flow on non-planar targets with material removal models to predict kerf cross-sections in single and multipass, overlapped and shallow-angle milling, all the way from shallow to deep pockets.

  • Kerf profile and material removal rate prediction
  • Jet flow dynamics on evolving, non-planar surfaces
  • PINNs and reduced-order models for kerf geometry
  • Micro-abrasive waterjet microchannels and submerged milling
  • In-situ machine-vision reconstruction of milled surfaces
Thread 2

Model-driven sequential machining

Published 2025

Abrasive waterjet trepanning planned by a through-kerf prediction model, which treats the kerf profile as a generatrix to predict and correct hole radius, cylindricity and taper. The same idea extends to controlled freeform surfaces by ultra-high-pressure waterjets for bioimplants.

Thread 3

Machine tool thermal compensation & dynamics

TRL 4

High-fidelity simulation combined with PINN-driven thermal reconstruction predicts tool-point error in air-cooled motorized spindles in real time from a few sensors. We also work on cyber-physical spindles with variable preload, high-speed spindle dynamics and passively damped boring bars.

  • Heat generation in rolling bearings
  • Preload-aware thermal error models
  • Coupled force-temperature PINNs
  • Chatter suppression with passive damping
Thread 4

Hybrid machine tools

New at IITH

A tabletop hybrid machining centre with a conventional milling spindle and abrasive waterjet or suspension-jet modules, with in-house CAM that plans toolpaths for both processes.

Thread 5

Abrasive flow nanofinishing

Foundation

Viscoelastic medium modelling and nano surface roughness simulation for abrasive flow finishing of macro-to-micro holes and microslots in surgical stainless steel (SS 316), and finishing of sculptured orthopaedic implants.

Thread 6

PINNs for multiphysics manufacturing

Methods

Physics-informed learning, surrogate and reduced-order models for thermo-mechanical and flow problems in manufacturing, including resistance spot welding, gas-solid multiphase flow and visco-elastic fluid flow, developed with the automotive industry.

I am an Assistant Professor in the Department of Mechanical and Aerospace Engineering, IIT Hyderabad. Prior to joining IITH, I worked as a Senior Engineer at TCS-General Motors, and earlier as a Postdoctoral Fellow at IITM Pravartak Technologies Foundation, IIT Madras Research Park.

In our research, we combine first-principles and physics-informed AI modelling with digital twins, cyber-physical systems and IIoT to monitor and control conventional and unconventional machining processes and machine tools, with applications in aerospace, defence, automotive and biomedical manufacturing. Courses at IIT Hyderabad

UG elective · 3 credits · B.Tech III / IV

Industrial Automation

Jan 2027 semester

Principles, systems and technologies of automation, so that students can design, analyse and implement automated manufacturing systems. Prepares students for industry needs in manufacturing, automation and robotics.

Topics
  • Types of automation: fixed, programmable and flexible
  • Automated flow lines, workpart transport and transfer mechanisms
  • Automated assembly: feeders, part orienting devices, design for automated assembly
  • Sensors for automated manufacturing systems
  • Drives and mechanisms: ball screws, linear bearings, cams; hydraulic, pneumatic and robotic transfer
  • Industry 4.0 and digital twins
